Case studies

The difference between books that look fine — and books that are fine.

Client names stay confidential. The outcomes speak for themselves.

Lender package · Cash flow

The cash report that was telling a nicer story than the bank.

  1. The problem

    A business preparing for financing had a cash flow report built from card-register data. On paper, every month looked comfortably positive.

  2. What I found

    The register missed transfers, loan payments, and timing differences. Rebuilt from the general ledger, several months had actually run negative.

  3. The fix

    I rebuilt the cash flow from the GL, tied each month to the bank statements, and documented every variance between the two versions.

  4. The result

    The owner saw the real picture before the lender did — and walked in with accurate numbers and a clear explanation instead of a surprise in underwriting.

E-commerce · Multi-year cleanup

Years of payouts booked as "sales" — and margins nobody could see.

  1. The problem

    An online seller's books were years behind. Marketplace and processor deposits had been recorded as net revenue, straight from the bank feed.

  2. What I found

    Fees, refunds, and chargebacks were buried inside the deposits. Revenue was understated, costs were invisible, and gross margin was a guess.

  3. The fix

    Clearing accounts for each processor, gross sales rebuilt from payout reports, and every account reconciled to its statement, year by year.

  4. The result

    True gross margin, visible for the first time — and closed, reconciled books ready for tax preparation, with nothing left to explain.

Real estate · Multi-entity

The intercompany balance that refused to zero out.

  1. The problem

    A group of property LLCs paid shared expenses from whichever account had cash. Nobody could say which entity owed which, or how much.

  2. What I found

    One-sided intercompany entries, expenses booked to the wrong entity, and due-to/due-from balances that drifted further apart every month.

  3. The fix

    An intercompany matrix, mirrored entries across entities, a documented allocation policy, and a monthly settlement routine.

  4. The result

    Intercompany balances that net to zero every close, reliable per-property P&Ls, and a consolidated view the owner can finally trust.
